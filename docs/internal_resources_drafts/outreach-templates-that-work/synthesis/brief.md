KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] Measure success by replies, not opens — expect low baseline reply rates; optimize for fewer, higher-context sends (Hunter.io 2025–2026 framing).
[GEM] Cap active targets per wave (~50–100 or smaller for students) to keep personalization real; mass blasts underperform smaller campaigns (Hunter.io).
[GEM] Subject-line scaffold for students: [School + major/Stack signal] + [specific team/product/role hook] + [Company] (aligns across CS cold-email guides; keeps skimmable).
[GEM] Body scaffold (~100–125 words): (1) 1–2 lines of proof you exist (project, course, club), (2) 2–3 lines proving you read *their* work (launch, post, talk, product), (3) one low-friction ask — e.g. 15-minute advice chat, not “please hire me” as the first line (student Medium + recruiter-style guides).
[GEM] Follow-up ladder: 2–3 short nudges that add one new detail (link, question, artifact) — not identical “just bumping” copy (Hunter.io + playbook-style networking follow-ups).
[GEM] Post-event template shape (summit/career fair): thank you + one specific moment or takeaway + one focused question — mirrors Cultivated Culture “follow up when networking” pattern and matches student recap evidence that human conversations outperform script-y recruiter pitches (LinkedIn Pulse recap).
[GEM] Lead with true shared context when available: “ColorStack member,” “Code2040 applicant/Fellow,” “MLT CP,” “met at [booth/session]” — increases relevance without inventing affinity (AfroTech ColorStack context; Code2040/MLT program pages).
Warm outreach beats cold when possible: ColorStack Slack/summits and Code2040/MLT programming are parallel channels that shrink the “fully cold” problem (Daily Northwestern; Code2040 programs page).
Use school/.edu or a clean custom domain for first impressions; avoid spammy tracking links if you want trust (Hunter.io trust section — adapt to student tooling budget).

DISCARDED
- “Network early / be passionate” without a send recipe — not actionable enough for this audience.
- Spray-and-pray hundreds of identical blasts — contradicted by 2025 reply-focused guidance and student time budgets.
- Over-optimizing send-time superstitions — Hunter argues message quality beats clock-watching; deprioritize for a 3-minute guide unless contradicted by stronger primary sources.
- Treating any single blog’s “35–40% response rate” as a promise — discard as headline bait without disclosed methodology.
- Long autobiography in email body — recruiter/skimming behavior makes >~150 words a liability for first touch (supporting consensus).

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE
Opening hook: You sent 20 emails and got silence — not because you’re “bad at networking,” but because the inbox rewards relevance, small batches, and a clear conversation ask.

Section 1: The reply-first mindset — one paragraph: baseline reply reality, small list discipline, when to use email vs LinkedIn DM.
Section 2: Who to message (pick one lane) — recruiter vs engineer vs alumni; one rule for finding the right title at big co vs startup.
Section 3: Copy-paste scaffolds (the core of a 3-minute article) — labeled blocks: (A) LinkedIn connection note, (B) cold email #1, (C) follow-up #1 with new detail, (D) post–career-fair / post–summit thank-you + question — each with bold [INSERT] fields only.
Section 4: Warm lanes in one tight paragraph — ColorStack (Slack/summits), Code2040 Fellows/Reverse Career Fair, MLT Career Prep — “use the true line in the first sentence when it applies.”
The mistake: Same template blasted wide with only the name changed — reads as spam and wastes cycles.
Close: Send 3 messages in 24 hours — each to a high-fit person, each with one provable line about their work and a 15-minute ask.

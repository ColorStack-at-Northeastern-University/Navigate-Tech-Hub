# Making the Most of Black and Latinx Tech Communities

You scroll LinkedIn in September and see people your age already posting airport selfies, summit badges, and “Day 1 at AfroTech” energy. You have no internship yet. Your GitHub is mostly class labs. Part of you wonders if those spaces are only for people who already have the whole package figured out.

They are not. The orgs that built those moments are built for you to enter early, mess up politely, and come back sharper. The mistake is treating them like logos you slap on a résumé once a year. The move is repeat touchpoints: prep, show up, follow up, stay in the Slack or newsletter loop until the next window opens.

## ColorStack, NSBE, and AfroTech are not interchangeable

**ColorStack** is built around computer science and closely related majors in the U.S. and Canada, with membership criteria tied to identity and allyship. If you are applying, read the member requirements like you read a syllabus. They care that your LinkedIn matches what you typed on the form: school, major, graduation year. A verified profile reads as a plus, not vanity. Fix the mismatch before you hit submit, not after someone flags it.

**NSBE** casts a wider net across Black STEM and engineering. Chapters give you community, leadership tracks, mentorship, and professional development on a campus rhythm. If your school has no chapter, you are not boxed out: **At-Large** membership still plugs you into national benefits while you figure out whether to help start something locally or stack another org alongside NSBE.

**AfroTech** is a conference-scale moment, not a student club. **Code2040** and **MLT** sit adjacent, not as drop-in replacements. Code2040’s Fellows Program is a longer cohort shape with mentorship, structured internship support, and equity curriculum; windows are real and they move. As of a recent check, the **2026 Fellows application was closed to new applicants** on the Fellows program subdomain, while some main-site FAQ copy still showed older calendar language. MLT’s college programming is its own shape (often multi-semester coaching and treks). If you are comparing programs, open each org’s current eligibility page instead of assuming one application covers the same thing as another.

Stack what fits your major, your campus reality, and how you actually want to spend energy this semester. You are allowed to be NSBE-heavy, ColorStack-curious, AfroTech-once-a-year, or the reverse.

## Put membership windows on the same calendar as registration

NSBE’s national membership year runs **August 1 through July 31**. If you join late in the cycle, national convention pricing and benefits can land differently than they would in August. Write that window next to your class registration dates like any other deadline. Late join still works; you just get a different price and benefit stack than someone who renewed in August.

ColorStack’s own recap of the **Stacked Up Summit** frames the event as relationship-building with partners **before** recruiting season kicks into high gear. Translation for first-years: you show up for reps and signal, not because your portfolio is already a museum piece. If you are waiting until you feel “technical enough” to engage, you are optimizing for the wrong gate. These communities reward consistent presence: a general body meeting, a virtual workshop, a resume review night. Pick one org to actually join this month, not five orgs you half-follow on Instagram.

## A first-year friendly AfroTech-style playbook

Upload your resume through the **Talent Infusion** onboarding flow first. AfroTech’s first-time tips push the resume book so employers can find you before the expo floor gets loud. Finish the upload early so it is a closed task before you are juggling hotel Wi-Fi and credential pickup.

Build a real schedule in the app, then leave holes on purpose. The same guidance that tells you to plan also warns that rigid minute-by-minute days crowd out the conversations you did not know you needed. Some of the best outcomes look like standing next to the right person in line because you left thirty minutes unscheduled.

Skip the rehearsed elevator pitch when everyone is fried by hour three of the expo hall. Use simple starters the student-facing tips suggest: a highlight from a session, a dream panel, the best advice you heard so far. When the room is tired, curiosity beats a script.

Expert and student voices both push quality over card volume. Send one or two thoughtful follow-ups before you leave town or right after, not fifty generic “great to connect” blasts.

Treat the floor as market intelligence. One first-time write-up frames expo time that way: see what peers at your level are building and what compensation bands look like while you are still figuring out if you are interview-ready. Go home and adjust your course plan and projects with what you saw instead of rumors from GroupMe.

## After the badge comes off

The week after a summit or conference is where most people ghost their own network. Pick a boring rhythm that still moves the needle: stay active in the org Slack or Discord, actually read the newsletter instead of archiving it, and aim for one coffee chat or async intro per month that references something specific you talked about. If your LinkedIn still does not match how you introduce yourself in rooms, fix that once and stop bleeding credibility on small mismatches.

Navigate Tech Hub sits in this ecosystem by design; the product is a **Northeastern x ColorStack** collaboration. Travel funding and chapter names still depend on your campus and what an editor can verify. Use your school’s org directory and your chapter leaders for money, buses, and reimbursement rules.

## Your next twenty-four hours

Pick **one** org: apply, renew, or join At-Large if the map says that is your path.

Make **one** profile fix so your public LinkedIn matches the next application you will submit.

Sign up for **one** date hold or newsletter (AfroTech registration interest, Code2040 student newsletter, ColorStack email) so the next window hits your inbox instead of your panic spiral.

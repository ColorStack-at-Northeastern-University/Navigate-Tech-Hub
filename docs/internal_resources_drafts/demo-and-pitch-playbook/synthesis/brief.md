DOMAIN ASSESSMENT

**Execution** (hackathons: demo + pitch under clock). Practitioner guides (MLH organizer/judge logistics, sponsor demo craft) and student retrospectives are equally useful; judge rubrics explain *what* earns points, MLH docs explain *how little time* you often have and common event formats.

SOURCES SEARCHED

1. **MLH Hackathon Organizer Guide — Judging Plan** ([guide.mlh.io](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan)) — Science-fair-style judging as default; submission **2-minute video should be a demo of the hack, not a slide deck**; closing-ceremony finalist slots often **~7 minutes per team** (~**3–5** presentation + **~2** changeover); judge debrief notes **~3 minutes per team** in expo flow; recommends in-person expo vs. siloed judging rooms for fairness.
2. **MLH — Best Practices for Giving an API Demo at a Hackathon** ([news.mlh.io](https://news.mlh.io/best-practices-for-giving-an-api-demo-at-a-hackathon-01-23-2023)) — Written for sponsors but transferable: **hook in the first ~30 seconds**; **3–5 minute cap** for “snippet” talks; **interactivity + clarity + novelty**; test risky interactive bits on a small group first; **dongles, offline demo, AV check**, ask organizers Wi‑Fi/firewall/projector/mic/time; **read the room** (phones, engagement) as a quality bar.
3. **Devpost — How to win a hackathon: Advice from seasoned judges** ([info.devpost.com](https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips)) — Judges care about **working product**, **originality**, **UX polish**, **learning**; avoid purely relative comparisons between teams; enthusiasm and completeness read as effort.
4. **Hackathon judging rubrics (e.g., HackHQ, organizer blogs)** — Typical weighting pattern: **innovation, technical execution, impact/value, presentation/communication** — implies the pitch must surface all four fast, not only architecture deep-dives.
5. **Student/practitioner blog — pitch timing sandwich** (e.g., Jorge Ferreiro recap citing judge-style guidance) — **Problem ~15s → live demo bulk ~90s → what you learned / challenges ~60s → leave air for Q&A** — useful scaffold when you have **~2–3 minutes** total; adapt to organizer’s stated limit.
6. **DEV posts** (e.g., AceHack pitch article, “surviving a hackathon” tips, DoraHacks submission shape) — **Rehearse timed run-throughs**; **hook + plain-language solution**; **fewer features shown clearly** beats feature dump; **screenshots or short video backup** if live demo fails; submission framing **what / why / who / how** without empty hype.
7. **AfroTech / ColorStack public hackathon outcomes** (winner announcements, LinkedIn recap posts) — Strong **examples of problem clarity and impact domains** (health, access, education); **light on step-by-step demo mechanics** — use for motivation and “what winning projects signal,” not as a primary how-to source.

KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] **Clocks are real and short**: plan for **expo-style ~3-minute judge rotations** *and* possibly **~3–5 minutes on stage** if you make finals — script **two lengths** (tight expo vs. slightly longer stage) from the same story.

[GEM] **Video / async demo ≠ finals pitch**: MLH-style guidance treats the **2-minute submission video as demo-first**; align your recorded artifact with **live choreography** (same golden path, same wording) so you are not re-inventing the story at the venue.

[GEM] **Audience pass (mandatory)**: before finals, one **non-technical listener** (friend outside CS) must restate **the problem, who it’s for, and what the app does in one sentence** after your pitch — if they can’t, **cut jargon and reorder** so the **first 20–30 seconds** pass the “hallway explanation” test (extends MLH “first 30 seconds” + general pitch advice).

[GEM] **Demo-first, not deck-first**: judges repeatedly reward **seeing working software** over extended startup-theater; open with **problem hook**, move quickly into **one golden-path walkthrough** (the flow you can complete asleep).

[GEM] **Golden path only**: pick **one user story** end-to-end; **pre-seeded accounts, preloaded tabs, enlarged fonts, DND on**, and a literal **printed run-of-show** with **who drives mouse / who speaks / who watches time** (from your draft scaffold — validated by execution-topic sources).

[GEM] **Failure plan on a sticky note**: **offline build, screenshots, or 30–60s screen recording** as fallback; **Wi‑Fi/firewall** question set for organizers; **AV check** if there is a stage — same failure modes appear in MLH + student posts.

[GEM] **Judge interruptions are normal in expo**: prepare **two “park lot” phrases** (“Great question — I’ll show that right after this 20-second flow” / “We scoped that out of the 24h build; here’s what we’d add next”) so you can **finish the demo arc** without sounding dismissive.

Supporting point: Rubrics mean you should **explicitly name impact** (who benefits) even when you are proud of the stack — **one plain-English impact line** closes the loop for non-SWE judges.

Supporting point: **Rehearse out loud** more than once; time **with** handoffs and **with** a single-speaker backup if someone is unavailable.

DISCARDED

- Generic “be passionate / network more” with no demo structure.
- Treating every hackathon like a **VC pitch** (long TAM slides) when the event format is **demo + short Q&A**.
- Overloaded feature tours that assume judges track **five** flows — contradicts “one golden path” consensus.
- Reddit-first sourcing (per skill: no direct Reddit search).

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE

Opening hook: Finals are where **working hacks lose** because the **first twenty seconds** were never rehearsed and the demo **assumes the judge already cares**.

Section 1: **Know your format** — expo table vs. stage finals; **match length** to organizer limits; **submission video vs. live** — same story.

Section 2: **The timed script** — hook → **golden-path demo** → **one technical credibility line** → **impact line** → team/ask; **two-length variants** (expo vs. stage).

Section 3: **Demo choreography** — roles, screen hygiene, backups, **failure plan**; **MLH-style prep questions** for Wi‑Fi/AV.

Section 4: **Judge + audience lens** — rubric categories translated into “what to show”; **mandatory non-technical audience pass**; handling **interruptions**.

The mistake: **Building until the buzzer** and **winging the story** — the limit is rarely code at finals; it’s **attention and clarity**.

Close: In the next **24 hours**, run **one timed dry run** with **golden path + backups + roles**, then **one non-technical listener pass**; write down **three lines** you will not improvise away from.

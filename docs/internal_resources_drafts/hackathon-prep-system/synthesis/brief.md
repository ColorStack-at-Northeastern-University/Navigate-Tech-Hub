KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] This week, open the hackathon’s Devpost “My Projects” draft and pre-fill what you can before the final crunch: add teammates (emails/invite link), project name + tagline, plan a 3:2 gallery thumbnail (JPG/PNG/GIF, 5 MB max), and list “Built with” tags including any required sponsor tools — Devpost submission steps explicitly require these and they routinely steal the last hours. (Devpost Help Center — Know Your Submission Steps)

[GEM] If the hackathon overview shows Devpost’s eligibility-review icon, aim to submit at least one week early so organizers can flag baseline issues (wrong video host, missing license/repo visibility, etc.) with time to revise — the help center ties early submit to that review path. (Devpost Help Center — Know Your Submission Steps)

[GEM] Before you brainstorm build ideas, block 30–60 minutes to read rules, required technologies/theme, and judging criteria; Devpost’s planning article frames this as the gate that prevents building the wrong thing. (Devpost Help Center — Tips for Planning Your Project)

[GEM] Team formation system for the week before: post in Participants tab / Discord with a one-liner on your skills + whether you have a seed idea; intentionally diversify roles (frontend, fuller-stack, design, PM-ish coordination); divide tasks by stated strengths; set realistic milestones if teammates have jobs/classes. (Devpost Help Center — Teamwork tips)

[GEM] Packing and venue safety (in-person): MLH’s packing guide’s “always quartet” is jacket/layers, reusable water bottle, noise-canceling headphones (or cheap earplugs), deodorant; never leave your laptop unattended; for meals, rotate who stays with gear; avoid a stuffed backpack (swag needs space). (MLH — Ultimate Hackathon Packing Guide)

[GEM] Laptop prep this week: download IDE/GitHub Desktop and heavy SDKs before doors open — MLH notes venue Wi‑Fi often degrades during the event. (MLH — Ultimate Hackathon Packing Guide)

[GEM] Execution checklist aligned to Devpost’s build list: explicitly assign owners for backend structure, frontend/UI, submission narrative, and demo video early — their planning article lists these as parallel workstreams that get underestimated. (Devpost Help Center — Tips for Planning Your Project)

[GEM] For Black/Latinx first-years wary of “everyone else knows more,” prioritize beginner-friendly and community-foregrounded hackathons when possible (example pattern: hybrid/beginner events marketed through chapters—e.g., Color Hacks + Northwestern NSBE on Devpost) and pair with org community (ColorStack-style mentorship/Slack) for team-finding and moral support; treat AfroTech-style resources as adjacent (conference navigation), not interchangeable with a 24–36h build sprint. (Devpost event page; Daily Northwestern / ColorStack reporting; AfroTech first-time attendee page — scope carefully)

[GEM] Student retrospectives: prep 2–3 idea tiers (ambitious / moderate / simple fallback), wireframe/schema before coding when possible, and favor familiar stacks over learning a new framework during the clock. (Dev.to beginner/rookie hackathon articles — multiple authors, consistent themes)

[Finding — supporting point] MLH’s “what to expect” framing: most hackathons cap teams around four people; expect a short judge conversation (~5 minutes cited) and code/product scrutiny varies; outcome goals include skills + relationships, not only placement. (MLH — What To Expect At A Hackathon)

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE
Opening hook: The real failure mode isn’t code—it’s the last 90 minutes fighting Devpost fields, video hosting, and merge conflicts while someone still hasn’t read the sponsor’s required API.

Section 1: Pick the right on-ramp (event + people) — beginner-friendly signals, chapter/org paths (including identity-affirming event patterns), where to find teams (Participants/Discord), and what “roles” means for a four-person cap.

Section 2: Pre-event team contract + technical preflight — tie-breaker for decisions, skill map (who owns deploy/demo/repo), laptop/tooling downloads, Git comfort, and a three-tier idea fallback.

Section 3: Time-boxed build + integration checkpoints — parallel workstreams (code vs submission narrative vs video), when to cut scope, and sleep/food realism (esp. first-year stamina).

Section 4: Submission as a workstream — field-by-field checklist (thumbnail spec, public YouTube/Vimeo demo, public repo + license), proofread pass, early submit if eligibility review applies.

The mistake: Building until the deadline and treating Devpost/video/repo as an afterthought.

Close: In the next 24 hours, register + join the community channel, start the Devpost draft, and schedule a 30-minute rules/criteria read with your team or future teammates.

KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] **Minute 0–5: triage the whole screen, not the first problem.** Skim every prompt, constraints, and section weights if shown; label each item easy/medium/unknown; decide order before deep work. (Aligns CodeSignal “move on / come back” guidance + DEV skim-first pattern.)

[GEM] **Bank a clean win early.** On multi-problem OAs, finish the most tractable item first to lock partial credit and calm the timer anxiety spiral—especially for underclassmen on unfamiliar UIs.

[GEM] **Use the platform’s tests and samples as the real spec.** When wording is dense, read sample I/O and failing/passing tests first to infer edge cases and stop rereading prose. (Medium “platform assessments” thread.)

[GEM] **One full dress rehearsal on the same vendor family (HackerRank, CodeSignal, etc.).** Run a practice or sample timed set to internalize: run button, custom input, language switch limits, and how hints/debugger work—so the first 10 minutes aren’t “paying tuition” on the tool.

[GEM] **Explicit move-on rule.** If an item’s approach isn’t emerging after a bounded slice of time (pick a personal number in the article, e.g., 10–15 minutes), switch tasks or downgrade to brute force / partial passes rather than zero output—matches CodeSignal checklist and student OA writeups.

[GEM] **Halfway clock check.** At 50% time remaining, rebalance: list what’s unsolved, drop perfectionism on the hardest, ensure at least one runnable submission per required part if the format allows.

[GEM] **Language and lookup policy are part of strategy.** Choose the language you can type fastest in; before Start, confirm what’s allowed (syntax docs vs. broader search vs. AI assistant). CodeSignal Pre-Screen explicitly allows syntax research online—other invites may not; the article should force a literal read of the invite/rules screen.

[GEM] **If the assessment is AI-assisted, behave like production:** explain your plan, ask targeted questions (edge cases, complexity), verify suggestions, and avoid “paste the whole solution” patterns—HackerRank documents logged AI transcripts for reviewers.

[GEM] **Underclassman UX: reduce ambient risk.** Plug in, close notifications, clear desk, and schedule when rested; CodeSignal cites sleep/performance correlation as a practical lever students can control.

Supporting point — Proctoring/watermarks/multi-monitor rules vary by employer; students should assume they’re being compared under strict integrity tooling and avoid improvising environment hacks.

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE
Opening hook: The timed screen isn’t testing only algorithms—it’s testing whether you can ship runnable answers before the clock eats your judgment.

Section 1: **Before you click Start** — Invite parsing: duration, sections, language, AI/proctoring, allowed resources, device rules; schedule the attempt when you’re sharp.

Section 2: **First five minutes: read everything, pick order** — Whole-exam triage, point budgets, “win first” sequencing, bookmark/revisit habit.

Section 3: **Execution loop under time** — Tests-as-spec, brute-force-first upgrade, move-on rule, halfway rebalance, last-10-minutes priority (run vs. polish vs. edge cases).

Section 4: **Platform realities (HackerRank / CodeSignal-shaped)** — Practice flow, IDE familiarity, when syntax lookup is allowed, AI-transcript awareness where applicable.

Section 5: **After the screen** — Expect sparse feedback; when follow-up is appropriate; mental reset if you need a retake or another company.

The mistake: Treating problem 1 like the whole exam—perfecting one item while leaving others blank.

Close: **24-hour action:** book one timed practice session in the same environment as your next invite, using the 0–5 minute triage checklist once for real.

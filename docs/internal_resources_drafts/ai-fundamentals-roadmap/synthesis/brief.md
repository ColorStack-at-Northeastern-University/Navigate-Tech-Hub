SOURCES SEARCHED
1. **Eaton & Epstein (AAAI-24), “Artificial Intelligence in the CS2023 Undergraduate Computer Science Curriculum”** — CS2023 integrates AI as a full knowledge area (KA) among 17 KAs; AI/ML now appears across SE, OS, networking, databases, etc.; sunflower model shows CS Core vs KA Core vs electives; KAs are not 1:1 with courses; AAAI joined ACM/IEEE for this revision. Contributes official framing for what “AI in a CS degree” means and why prerequisites are threaded through math/stats (MSF) and other cores.
2. **ACM / IEEE-CS CS2023 reports & Communications coverage** — Confirms global stakeholder input scale and that AI is a sustained curricular pillar (supporting detail for inventory-aligned advice: check departmental instantiation of CS Core + AI depth).
3. **Candon et al. (AAAI-25), “Artificial Intelligence for Future Presidents” (Yale)** — “No math, no programming” literacy course for informed use (driver’s-license analogy); literate users vs builders; policy/ethics engagement. Useful distinction for students choosing between an AI literacy elective vs a technical ML/AI builder sequence.
4. **UT Austin one-credit AI literacy course paper (NSF PAR / AAAI proceedings family)** — Rapid-deployment, broad-audience literacy module; guest lectures; flexible async access; pre/post literacy measures. Supports recommendation to **use** institution-wide literacy offerings as parallel track to CS major depth.
5. **“AI Literacy for Hispanic-Serving Institution (HSI) Students” (AAAI Symposium)** — Research emphasis on faculty-mentored undergraduate research and hands-on activities supporting Hispanic students in AI literacy contexts. Supports article angle: pair coursework with structured research/hands-on blocks where available.
6. **Equity / pipeline framing (Kapor “Leaky Tech Pipeline,” Harlem STEM Up, NSF “Black and Latine youth” AI/STEM engagement)** — Structural access gaps; AI literacy as justice-relevant; culturally sustaining pedagogy themes in CS. Use to justify **why** critical AI literacy (limits, bias, governance) belongs in the same roadmap as math/programming prerequisites—not optional “soft” content.
7. **ColorStack + AfroTech coverage** — Nonprofit mission: increase Black and Latinx CS graduates in technical careers; boot camps, summits, Slack, workshops, career fairs; **SambaNova scholarship** explicitly targets students who completed ML coursework at B− or better with mentorship/interview pathways; NSBE convention scholarships. Actionable community + credential checkpoints for students pursuing ML depth.
8. **Medium — self-study ML/AI roadmaps (e.g., Thu Vu, Halder, others in search results)** — Repeated pattern: Python → NumPy/Pandas/Matplotlib → supervised learning first → projects early; 30-day sprint vs slower path. Student voice for “what to do this month.”
9. **DEV — beginner AI/ML roadmaps (multiple authors)** — Reinforces taxonomy (AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL), problem types (supervised/unsupervised/RL), tool stack (scikit-learn, later PyTorch/TensorFlow), Colab/Kaggle. Student voice for weekly-level sequencing.

Eaton & Epstein stress KAs ≠ courses; the payoff is knowing *why* scattered requirements exist and which **one** elective unlocks depth next.

Student roadmaps (Medium/Dev.to consensus) align with CS2023’s emphasis on mathematical/statistical cores for builders—not because every AI job needs PhD math, but because **course gates** do.

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE
Opening hook: The confusion between **AI hype**, **AI literacy gen-ed**, and **CS major depth**—and why picking the wrong lane wastes a semester of prerequisites.

Section 1: **What “AI in CS” means now (CS2023 lens)** — AI as distributed knowledge area + ethics; KAs are not single courses; why ML prerequisites show up in math/stats/DS.

Section 2: **Choose your lane: literate user vs builder** — Yale/UT-style literacy outcomes vs technical ML path; how to tell which lane matches career intent this year.

Section 3: **Prerequisite map you can verify in the catalog** — MSF, data structures, prob/stats, linear algebra; what to register for next if ML is the target.

Section 4: **First projects that match hiring and coursework reality** — supervised learning first; one notebook, real metrics; avoid premature deep learning.

Section 5: **Community, scholarships, and equity-aware literacy** — ColorStack/NSBE/AfroTech-adjacent signals; bias and governance as literacy requirements for Black/Latinx students disproportionately affected by automated systems (pipeline + HSI literature).

The mistake: Treating “AI fundamentals” as **one course title** instead of a **thread** (technical + ethical + institutional) and skipping the prerequisite graph until registration locks.

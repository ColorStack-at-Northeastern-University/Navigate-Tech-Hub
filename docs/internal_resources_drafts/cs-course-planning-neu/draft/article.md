# CS Course Planning for Northeastern Students

You can spend a week shuffling electives and still lock yourself out of a course you need the next year. The real friction is prerequisite chains, catalog-year rules that do not track random blog posts, and co-op blocks where you are not in lecture halls. This guide does not paste a frozen course map. Requirements shift by program and entry year. It gives you a method tied to official Northeastern and Khoury pages; anything that still needs a live-catalog confirmation stays in a [FACT CHECK] tag until an editor clears it.

## Official requirements live on four sites, not on Reddit

Treat these as your anchors. Open them in tabs before you draw a single semester box:

- Northeastern academic catalog (program pages): [https://catalog.northeastern.edu/](https://catalog.northeastern.edu/)
- Khoury College (programs and student-facing sections): [https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/)
- Khoury’s hub for current undergraduates (advising and academic-policy entry point; subpaths change): [https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/current-undergraduate-students/](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/current-undergraduate-students/)
- University Registrar (records and policy): [https://registrar.northeastern.edu/](https://registrar.northeastern.edu/)

Your degree is evaluated against your **catalog year** (rules tied to your entry term), not the shiniest page on the site this week. Forwarded PDFs, screenshots, and generic “CS roadmaps” for schools with no co-ops are weak next to your official program page for that year.

Before you build a grid, get transfer and placement credit onto the record you will actually register against. A plan built around courses you might not need is a different plan. ## Build a semester grid from a prerequisite graph, not from a vibes list

Take required courses from your official program page and draw prerequisite arrows yourself. Lists hide chokepoints; graphs show them. Mark:

1. Nodes that unlock several downstream courses (critical path; one slip can cost a year, not one term).
2. Fall-only or spring-only courses. [FACT CHECK: confirm patterns in the live schedule of classes for your catalog year.]
3. Elective lanes (systems, ML, theory, security, HCI, and similar) with their own prereq ladders, not just the core.
4. Non-CS requirements (math, writing, breadth rules). [FACT CHECK: confirm current general-education or NUpath-style names and buckets for your program; labels change.]
5. Catalog lines marked “recommended” instead of “required.” Treat those as soft prereqs until advising documents a skip.

Drop courses into a fall/spring grid per year; leave cells empty for co-op terms when you are not in class. [FACT CHECK: confirm the typical co-op cycle for your Khoury program and entry year with official Khoury co-op materials and your co-op advisor. Do not copy an upperclassman’s rhythm without verifying it applies to you.]

Three constraints that stay true across cycles:

- Work backward from the first semester you will apply for co-op, not forward from orientation. Anything that must be done before that application belongs earlier on the graph than feels comfortable.
- If a required course is only offered in a term where you are scheduled on co-op, you have manufactured a wait. Catch that collision on paper, not during add/drop.
- The semester right after a co-op block is not a free slot. Fatigue is real, and recruiting for the next cycle starts quickly. Avoid stacking every project-heavy course into that rebound term by default.

## Electives and when to specialize

Electives turn into random shopping if you never pick a lane. Keep the lane loose enough to pivot; let the graph dictate timing for depth courses. For how to pick the lane itself, use the internal resource **selecting-electives-intentionally** (Selecting electives intentionally). For sequencing, two rules are enough:

- If an elective you want has a prereq chain, pull the earliest link on that chain forward in the grid. Popular sections fill; “I will take it junior year” fails when junior year is full.
- Minors and concentrations look attractive sophomore year. Before you declare, read both the official requirements and the offering pattern for any capstone or concentration-only courses. [FACT CHECK: confirm the current declaration process and any concentration-specific offering windows for your catalog year.]

## Where to verify before you register

A plan you wrote alone is still a draft. Confirm it against:

- Your assigned Khoury academic advisor, reached through the current undergraduate hub above. [FACT CHECK: confirm the current booking workflow and how students identify their assigned advisor in your program.]
- The official degree audit workflow the Registrar documents for your entry year. Portal labels change; [FACT CHECK: confirm the current student-facing name and access path for Northeastern’s degree audit tool on https://registrar.northeastern.edu/, not from this article or forums.]
- The catalog program page for your catalog year, not the latest catalog if you matriculated earlier.

Bring a marked-up grid and three specific questions. “Here is my plan; I am unsure at these prereq junctions” earns better answers than “what should I take?”

## The mistake to avoid

Planning from national CS roadmaps, anonymous forum threads, or “what everyone takes” stories. Those can be useful for culture and difficulty rumors. They are not authoritative for your requirements. A friend who entered two years earlier may be on different rules. If someone says “everyone takes course X second semester,” treat it as a hypothesis. Verify course code, requirement status, and restriction under your catalog year on the official program page before you register.

## What you should leave this with

You want a semester grid keyed to your graph (co-op blocks blank on purpose), the same four official URLs in bookmarks, and an “unplanned” list: anything required or desired that still lacks a target semester. That list is what you bring to advising.

## A 24-hour action

Open your catalog program page for your entry year beside whatever progress view your student portal shows. Mark each requirement **done**, **in progress**, **planned**, or **unplanned**. For unplanned lines, add a semester that fits the offering pattern or write one precise question to ask on official channels, not in a group chat.

KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] **Stage-specific evaluators, stage-specific questions:** Recruiter/coordinator screen is not testing whether you can implement Dijkstra — it tests whether you’re plausible enough to spend expensive engineering hours on (clarity, logistics, basic fit, sometimes visa/location). Technical screens test reasoning + implementation under time; behavioral rounds test evidence of how you work with people and messy priorities.

[GEM] **Communication is evaluated everywhere** — but what “good” means shifts: concise scheduling emails early; narrated thinking mid-problem later; structured stories with outcomes in behavioral.

[GEM] **Interest specificity** shows up early: why this team/product/stack, not generic praise — recruiter guides emphasize this as a differentiator when technical depth is still thin for first-years.

[GEM] **Technical rounds** (even intern-level) reward: clarifying questions, sensible brute-force before optimization, testing ideas, clean enough code, and honest tradeoff talk — not only “getting the answer.”

[GEM] **Behavioral rounds** reward: concrete stories with your actions (not team hand-waving), outcomes, and reflection; overlap with Navigate’s `behavioral-story-bank` — link rather than re-teach STAR from scratch.

[GEM] **Panel / loop** stages often mix behavioral + technical + “culture” interviewers — each round may optimize for one slice; debrief combines signals students never see directly. [FACT CHECK: “hiring committee” mechanics are company-specific.]

Supporting point: OA / timed platforms emphasize speed and pattern recognition — pointer to `online-assessment-strategy` for tactics; here, name **what signal** the firm is buying (reliability under clock).

Supporting point: HM (hiring manager) conversations for interns are not universal but when present tilt toward scope, initiative, learning agility.

DISCARDED

- “They only care about LeetCode” — collapses distinct stages and misallocates prep time.
- Generic “be confident” without naming which stage confidence is demonstrated (communication vs coding).
- Treating behavioral as optional for SWE — many loops still fail candidates on teamwork/communication even with adequate code.

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE

Opening hook: You prepared like every round was a hard LeetCode contest, then lost on a recruiter screen or a “tell me about a conflict” question — because each stage scores a different slice of you.

Section 1: Map the usual cast of rounds (recruiter → OA optional → technical → behavioral → panel) with **who is in the room** and **what they’re trying to learn**.

Section 2: **Early gates** — recruiter/coordinator: signals, failure modes (vague interest, rambling, no answer for “why this role”).

Section 3: **Technical gates** — what strong looks like beyond “finished the problem” (tests, edge cases, complexity discussion).

Section 4: **Behavioral / experience gates** — competencies and evidence; link `behavioral-story-bank`.

Section 5 (short): **Community / conference micro-interactions** — evaluation under time pressure (AfroTech/ColorStack context), without overstating.

The mistake: One prep stack for every stage — usually over-rotating on algorithms and under-rotating on articulation, stories, and logistics professionalism.

Close: In the next 24 hours, pick **one upcoming stage** for your next interview (or mock) and write five bullets: what they’re scoring, what proof you’ll bring, one question you’ll ask them — no new grinding until that map is explicit.

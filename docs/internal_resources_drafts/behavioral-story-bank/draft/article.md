# Behavioral Interview Story Bank with STAR

## Editor meta

- Silent-read word budget: about 900–1,100 words.
- Voice: `.cursor/rules/navigate-writing.mdc`. Do not define STAR from scratch like the reader never heard of it; they are in the major.
- Volatility **low**: prompts are stable; company rubrics shift slightly.

## Opening (replace with friction-led prose)

Behavioral answers go flat when they are written like hero fanfiction. Build a small bank of true stories you can rotate without memorizing a script.

## What a story bank buys you

- Fewer panic rewrites the night before.
- Cleaner handoffs between “conflict,” “failure,” and “leadership” without recycling the same fake arc.

## Picking 5–7 anchor stories

- Class, work, club, team project: mix sources so you are not one-note.
- What counts as “leadership” when you had no title.

## STAR without sounding like a paragraph factory

- Situation: one sentence max most of the time.
- Task vs action: where students blend them and lose credit.

## Mapping stories to prompt clusters

- Ownership, ambiguity, conflict, deadline pressure, mistakes, feedback you disagreed with.
- **Your bullets:** paste your cluster list if you standardize on a framework.

## Practice that does not turn you into a robot

- Record 60 seconds, listen for “and then and then,” cut.
- Partner drill: one follow-up question per story.

## Red flags interviewers hear constantly

- Everyone learns “the real lesson.” Show the behavior change, not the moral.

## Your notes / guided bullets

- Write working titles for 3 draft stories you will finish before publish.

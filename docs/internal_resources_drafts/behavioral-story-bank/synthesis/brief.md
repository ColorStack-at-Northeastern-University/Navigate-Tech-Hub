KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] Build a **projects/experiences × behavioral prompts** matrix before polishing prose: list 3–6 anchor experiences (courses, group projects, CodeDay Labs / hackathons, jobs, org leadership), then map common questions (conflict, mistake, ambiguity, prioritization, difficult teammate, learning fast) to each row using STAR. Byte by Byte references Gayle Laakmann McDowell’s **Interview Preparation Grid** and points to a downloadable spreadsheet (`behavior_preparation_grid.xlsx` via thegoogleresume.com) — replicate the idea in Notion/Sheets if you do not use Excel.

[GEM] Keep each delivered story **under ~2 minutes**; if you run long, cut Situation/Task and remove extra technical depth (Byte by Byte). Put **most sentences in Action**; open with 1–2 sentences of context, close Result with outcome and a crisp lesson or metric when possible (aligns with Levels.fyi STAR sketch and multiple STAR guides’ “Action-heavy” guidance).

[GEM] **Reuse a small set of stories** across questions: Levels.fyi explicitly recommends not memorizing one story per question; instead prepare a handful of strong narratives you can **re-angle** to fit different prompts.

[GEM] Run a **company pass** before interviews you have in the next week: read About/mission plus **one recent news item**, then draft two bullets for “Why this company?” that tie to what they say they value (Levels.fyi). Optionally cross-check Amazon LP-style or similar public value frameworks when interviewing at companies known for them (Levels.fyi links).

[GEM] **Mine non-internship evidence** intentionally: group projects, tutoring/TA, customer-facing jobs, sports/performing arts teams, family responsibilities — Byte by Byte’s “no relevant experience” FAQ; this is critical for first-year students who lack a SWE internship.

[GEM] **Practice out loud** with one peer or mentor mock this week; debrief using Levels.fyi’s lens — did each answer give the listener a clear **“data point”** (decision quality, ownership, collaboration signal) they could repeat in a hiring debrief?

[GEM] When you draft each story, **highlight verbs you personally did**; default to **“I” for your decisions and execution**, and only use “we” to establish context before zooming into your slice (Byte by Byte).

Supporting point — Tag each story in your bank with 2–4 competencies (leadership without authority, conflict, ambiguity, failure + learning, stakeholder communication, etc.) so you can filter quickly mid-interview (JobMentis-style story bank concept).

Supporting point — Expect internship screens to cluster around teamwork, deadlines, challenge/learning, and communication; map your bank to those clusters early (Medium internship questions roundup).

Supporting point — Programs serving Black and Latinx CS talent (e.g., MLT Career Prep testimonials, SEO Tech Developer’s stated prep stack) emphasize **structured practice and coaching**; treat community program mocks and assigned prep as production rehearsals, not separate from your personal story bank.

Supporting point — CodeDay Labs positions **career panels and recruiting literacy** alongside technical work; stories can cite OSS scope, mentor feedback, and cross-student collaboration as Situation/Task sources.

DISCARDED

- **Ultra-long STAR answers (e.g., 5–10 minutes)** suggested in some student posts — conflicts with “never more than two minutes” practitioner guidance; for this audience, optimize for concise debrief-friendly answers first.
- **Unverified performance statistics** in some commercial STAR blogs (e.g., predictive validity percentages, offer-rate lifts) — cut as credibility noise; keep behavioral mechanisms instead.
- **Generic motivation / “just network”** — no mapping to prompts or STAR structure.
- **Assuming managerial or internship-only examples** — inappropriate for first-year inventory without the Byte by Byte-style expansion to campus/life evidence.

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE
Opening hook: The gap between “I’ve done stuff” and “I can’t say it cleanly in 90 seconds” before a screen or career fair.

Section 1: What interviewers are extracting — **Why this matters for debriefs** (Levels.fyi “data points” framing).

Section 2: STAR as a timeboxed habit — **2-minute shape**, Action-heavy, “I”-led ownership; **common mistake callouts** (rambling, vague “we,” not answering the question) from practitioner sources.

Section 3: The story bank system — matrix/tags, **6–10** strong stories for interns/first-year (volume guidance synthesized from Levels.fyi “few stories” + JobMentis-style bank idea), competency labels, and **first-year evidence sources** (classes, orgs, CodeDay Labs, work).

Section 4: Prompt coverage kit — group **Levels.fyi sample questions** + internship-common questions; show **one story → three prompts** remapping.

Section 5: **Navigate kickoff ties** — where structured practice shows up (CodeDay Labs career programming; MLT/SEO-style program emphasis on mocks); link outward only when it helps the reader find **practice**, not to promote orgs.

The mistake: Building one-off essay answers instead of a **reusable, tagged bank**; or preparing stories that cannot survive a time limit.

Close: In the next 24 hours, list five experiences, pick three prompts each, and **record** one 90-second take; then swap mocks with a peer.

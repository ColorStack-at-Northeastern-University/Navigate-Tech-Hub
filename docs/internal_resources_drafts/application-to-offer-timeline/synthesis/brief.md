KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] Think in **stages**, not one story every company tells the same way: common buckets are (1) application + resume/ATS, (2) recruiter or coordinator screen, (3) online assessment / take-home (optional), (4) technical screen(s), (5) “onsite” / virtual loop / panel, (6) debrief / decision, (7) verbal offer → written offer → compliance/onboarding. Order and count vary — say so plainly.

[GEM] **Silence and ghosting are part of the timeline** — practical student action is tracking where each application sits and normalizing that “no news” is not always a personal verdict. (Inferential from process guides + student experience; avoid fake stats.)

[GEM] **Seasonality** matters for summer internships: many guides push starting applications in late summer / early fall of the prior academic year for the next summer; competitive pipelines fill early. [FACT CHECK: current cycle for your target year; Northeastern co-op calendars may differ — calibrate in draft if needed.]

[GEM] **First contact** might be inbound (recruiter DM/email) or outbound (you applied) — timeline branches: event/conference follow-up often accelerates scheduling vs cold portal apply.

[GEM] Between stages, **scheduling and responsiveness** are their own mini-phase (time zones, calendar tools, same-day reply norms without panic-sending).

[GEM] Offer stage has substeps students forget: verbal outline → written letter → deadlines to accept/decline → possible background check; negotiation (if any) is a separate guide (`negotiating-your-first-offer`).

Supporting point: FAANG-oriented timeline posts emphasize multiple technical rounds; smaller companies may do one strong technical conversation — calibrate expectations by employer type.

Supporting point: CodeDay’s recruiter-non-technical framing helps explain **why** early stages feel arbitrary or fast.

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE

Opening hook: You are refreshing your inbox expecting a single straight line from “submitted” to “congrats,” and instead you get parallel threads, random skips, and long gaps — the pipeline only makes sense after you name the stages.

Section 1: The canonical stages (application through written offer) — diagram-style prose, explicit “your company may reorder or drop steps.”

Section 2: **Seasonality and volume** — when first-years usually enter the market; rolling vs batch; why early prep on resume/projects still matters before interviews (tie lightly to CodeDay gate).

Section 3: **Between-stage reality** — scheduling, OA platforms, waiting, follow-up boundaries; link forward to `online-assessment-strategy` where relevant.

Section 4: **Conference / community inflection points** — how AfroTech / ColorStack-style moments change velocity (high level, fact-checked).

The mistake: Treating every employer like the same FAANG blog post — then mis-preping (e.g., obsessing about system design when the next gate is OA + one coding screen).

Close: In the next 24 hours, list every open application with **current stage** + **next expected action** (your move vs theirs); fix any place you’re “waiting” without knowing what you’re waiting for.

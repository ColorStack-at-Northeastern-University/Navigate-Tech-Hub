KEY FINDINGS

[GEM] Design for a **6-second decision**: name + what kind of builder you are + primary stack visible **without scrolling**; assume the reader is pattern-matching, not exploring. Sources: ShowProof + checklist-style hiring posts.

[GEM] **Featured projects (2–4 strong) beat a long gallery**: each needs a one-line outcome, stack, live demo (if any), and source link; if the demo is broken or slow, it actively hurts you — fix or remove. Sources: hiring-manager checklists + student retrospectives.

[GEM] **Clarity beats novelty**: recruiters and many hiring managers reward “I can tell what this does and why it exists” over animation-heavy landing pages that bury substance. Sources: GitReady / Scale.jobs-style guidance + student “tutorial vs real project” writeups.

[GEM] **Proof on click-through**: README with setup, architecture one-liner, and your role; issues/PRs or commit history that show you drove changes; avoid empty repos that only exist as link bait. Sources: checklist posts + GitHub-forward student patterns.

DISCARDED

- “Use Three.js / flashy 3D hero” as default advice — optional polish only after content is clear; many first-years over-invest here.
- Generic “show passion” / “tell your story” without a project-evidence scaffold.
- Treating class projects as unmentionable — reframed: weak when only course codes; strong when written with problem, scope, and your decisions (mirrors resume brief).
- Unsourced survey percentages (“X% of recruiters…”) — keep qualitative unless primary data is verified at publish time.

RECOMMENDED STRUCTURE

Opening hook: You built a portfolio site, but the first reader gives it a **recruiter skim** — if they can’t answer “what do they build, with what, and where’s proof?” in one screen, they move on.

Section 1: **The skim path** — above-the-fold contract, F-pattern, mobile; what gets clicked first (projects, GitHub, demo).

Section 2: **Project cards that convert** — 2–4 features, required fields per project, demo policy, README/repo minimum viable proof; student-lesson emphasis on deployment and real constraints.

Section 3: **Site stack and ops** — simple hosting path (Pages/Vercel), custom domain optional, fast loads, broken-link audit; OSS templates as optional accelerators, not requirements.

Section 4: **Resume and LinkedIn alignment** — headline/stack match, featured order by target role, one URL everywhere; keyword honesty (Teal-style tailoring without stuffing).

Section 5 (short): **Where this audience shows it off** — fairs, applications, community summits — “portfolio + pitch” pairing; verified facts only.

The mistake: A beautiful landing page with vague project titles, dead demos, and repos that don’t show your work — you signal “presentation without engineering.”

Close: In the next 24 hours, fix or remove one broken demo, rewrite three project one-liners as **problem → what you built → stack**, and add your portfolio URL to your resume + LinkedIn with matching keywords.

This document serves as a reminder of what the navigate tech hub is and is not


The platform becomes a guided journey rather than a directory.

A first-semester student lands on the homepage, sees "Start Here," reads the roadmap article, and gets routed into a content path that tells them exactly what to do, in what order, at what time of year. They don't have to know what they're looking for — the platform knows and tells them.

The external resources section supports that journey rather than trying to be comprehensive. It points to the 20-30 tools and programs that matter most, with honest seasonal context, and hands off to better-maintained sources for everything else.

The internal articles are the core product. Synthesized, human-voiced, specific to Black and Latinx students at NEU, written at the right level for someone who just arrived. Over time those articles become the reason students share the platform — not "here's a list of links" but "this thing actually explained how recruiting works in a way that made sense to me."

The contribution flow means the community starts feeding it over time — students who went through the process suggesting resources, flagging dead links, eventually maybe contributing articles. You stop being the sole maintainer and become the editor.

**The end state:** a student who finds Navigate Tech Hub in September of freshman year has everything they need to navigate the next four years — what to build, when to apply, how to interview, where to find community — without having to piece it together from ten different Discord servers and Reddit threads. That's the product. That's what makes it worth building.
